# Founding Sponsorship and Research Collaboration

This page describes the proposed founding sponsorship and related research collaboration opportunities for the Babak AI Engineering Standard (BAES).

The initial request is **USD 40,000 in founding sponsorship** to support strategic collaboration, continued BAES development, and practical application of the standard. The proposed budget includes a defined allocation for the founder's **Research and Engineering Leadership**, alongside agreed research, engineering, and development activities.

This is a sponsorship proposal, not an equity investment, purchase of BAES intellectual property, or payment for a consulting project. The initial planning period is approximately six months and is a negotiable planning horizon, not an inflexible completion deadline.

Any commercial-use rights, institutional access, licensing, or other permissions must be negotiated separately and documented in writing. Sponsorship alone does not grant ownership, blanket exclusivity, unrestricted commercial rights, or control over BAES research findings, evaluation criteria, architecture, or standardization decisions.

Each opportunity is subject to a clearly defined scope, agreed deliverables, and transparent terms. Listing an opportunity does not imply an existing partnership, funding commitment, endorsement, or transfer of intellectual property.

The current name of BAES is not necessarily its final or unchangeable name. A sponsor formally designated as a Founding Sponsor or Principal Institutional Partner may submit a formal proposal for future naming; that proposal will receive substantial weight and priority in the review and decision-making process. This is a proposal right, not unilateral naming authority, and does not transfer ownership or control of BAES. Any such recognition and the final approval process must be documented separately.

## Opportunity 01 — Independent Evaluation of Human Authorization in AI-Assisted Engineering

**Status:** Open for Discussion  
**Current research phase:** Phase 1 — Active  
**Next research phase:** Phase 2 — Deferred and Conditional  
**Proposed programme duration:** Approximately six months  
**Proposed programme funding:** USD 40,000

### Objective

Support an independent, evidence-based evaluation of how human intent, authorization, delegated AI actions, available evidence, and human decisions can be represented and reconstructed in AI-assisted engineering work.

The proposed comparative scope includes BAES, NIST AI RMF 1.0, and ISO/IEC 42001:2023. Existing Phase 1 work is a bounded, criterion-conditioned assessment of public BAES text and the NIST AI RMF 1.0 Core. ISO/IEC 42001:2023 remains unreviewed at clause level (UL); no comprehensive comparison, conformity finding, or framework ranking is claimed.

The evaluation is designed to permit positive, negative, mixed, or inconclusive findings. It does not assume that BAES performs better than the comparison frameworks.

The current engagement prioritizes a bounded evidence-assessment task rather than attempting to establish universal validity across all domains.

### Current Research Scope

The project is organized into two distinct phases.

**Phase 1 — Evidence-Packet Construction and Investigator-Side Assessment**

**Status: Active**

Phase 1 remains active, but substantial investigator-side work has already been recorded. The current funding opportunity is for completion, stabilization, source/criteria review, and appropriately bounded reporting—not for presenting completed investigator-side work as a future independent evaluation.

**Completed to date (investigator-side):**

- Constructed the public synthetic S01-A and S01-B evidence variants, with common technical artifacts and distinct authorization records.
- Completed an investigator-side documentary reconstruction audit and recorded criterion-level assessments using the recovered historical C1–C20 set.
- Recorded packet-local technical reproduction and its exact source-version limitations.
- Produced an interim comparative synthesis with separate framework-text coverage, event-evidence sufficiency, and residual-ambiguity axes, including investigator-side adversarial calibration.

These are investigator-side records only. They are not independent second-coder verification, human-review results, or a frozen or independently validated evaluation. The original date/version provenance of the recovered historical C1–C20 set remains unverified.

**Remaining / planned Phase 1 work includes:**

1. Stabilizing and freezing the S01 evidence packets, baseline instruction, and evidence index.
2. Completing the remaining scenario and criteria consistency review; keep the recovered historical C1–C20 set distinct from the separate Proposed v0.1 criteria.
3. Obtaining independent second-coder verification if an appropriate reviewer is available. If not, report the findings explicitly as investigator-side work.
4. Finalizing the bounded comparative synthesis. The existing NIST analysis is limited to the AI RMF 1.0 Core. ISO/IEC 42001:2023 remains unreviewed at clause level (UL) because the complete final-edition clause text has not been examined under an authorized access route; no clause-level ISO conclusion is claimed.
5. Completing confidentiality, intellectual-property, licensing, privacy, and third-party-rights review of the public artifacts. The review to date is targeted, not exhaustive.
6. Completing the Phase 1 report and a limitations-first public summary.

The unit of assessment in Phase 1 is the evidence packet and its records, not human reviewer performance.

**Expected Phase 1 completion outputs:**

- Finalized evidence packets and evidence index, building on the existing investigator-side artifacts;
- Finalized mapping against the recovered historical C1–C20 set, with its unresolved provenance limitation stated;
- Updated investigator-side reconstruction and evidence-sufficiency assessment, with independent-review status explicit;
- Updated ambiguity register;
- Bounded comparative synthesis, with ISO/IEC 42001:2023 remaining UL at clause level unless authorized access changes;
- Phase 1 research report and appropriate public summary.

**Phase 2 — Human Reviewer Evaluation**

**Status: Deferred and Conditional**

Phase 2 is a proposed human-participant study. It is not part of the currently active assessment.

Any later human-review study would require a separately designed, held-out scenario and a protocol frozen before recruitment. The public proposal does not disclose non-public scenario details or reviewer-allocation design.

| Condition | Review approach | Decision evidence |
|---|---|---|
| A-R1 | Conventional review | Explicit traceable decision record |
| A-R2 | Conventional review | Artifact approval; specific informed authorization unresolved |
| B-R1 | Structured review | Explicit traceable decision record |
| B-R2 | Structured review | Artifact approval; specific informed authorization unresolved |

Each reviewer would receive one evidence packet only.

The public S01 scenario is not suitable for blinded confirmatory testing because its behavior and boundary-test result are already public. It may support exploratory usability work, but it will not be presented as a blinded confirmatory case.

Phase 2 can proceed only after:

- Phase 1 evidence packets and the evidence index are stabilized;
- Any non-public study materials are finalized, versioned, and protected by verified access controls;
- Private repository access controls and collaborator permissions are verified;
- The study protocol, reviewer assignment, instructions, coding, exposure rules, consent, privacy, and data-retention arrangements are defined and frozen;
- Independent reviewers are available.

The proposed target is eight independent reviewers, with two per condition. A minimum of four may support a limited feasibility pilot if all four conditions are represented. A smaller or incomplete sample would not support comparative condition-effect claims.

No human reviewer has been recruited or assessed to date, and no human-review data have been collected.

### Research Brief

The [Evaluation Brief](evaluation-brief.md) provides the detailed research question, phase-specific scope, comparison conditions, deliverables, proposed timeline, indicative budget, and independence safeguards.

The brief is a research proposal, not a frozen protocol, preregistered study, completed evaluation, or claim of validation.

### Proposed Scope of Collaboration

The immediate collaboration opportunity concerns completion and independent scrutiny of Phase 1. It may include:

1. Supporting final stabilization and version control of the S01 evidence packets and evidence index.
2. Reviewing the recovered historical C1–C20 criteria and the separate Proposed v0.1 set without merging them.
3. Providing independent second-coder verification, if an appropriate reviewer is available.
4. Reviewing the existing investigator-side reconstruction audit and traceability approach.
5. Reviewing the source-bounded comparative synthesis, including the explicit ISO/IEC 42001 access limitation.
6. Completing publication-boundary, confidentiality, licensing, and third-party-rights checks.
7. Supporting the final Phase 1 report and documentation of remaining uncertainties.

Support for Phase 2 may be discussed separately, subject to completion of its prerequisites and an agreed research arrangement.

The final scope, responsibilities, deliverables, and acceptance criteria will be agreed with the funding or research partner before work begins.

### Expected Deliverables

**Phase 1 — Completion Deliverables**

- Finalized evidence packets and evidence index;
- Finalized investigator-side C1–C20 mapping using the recovered historical criteria, with provenance limitations stated;
- Independent second-coder verification report, only if an independent reviewer is secured; otherwise, an explicit statement that verification remains outstanding;
- Finalized reconstruction and evidence-sufficiency assessment, with scope limits;
- Updated ambiguity register;
- Bounded comparative synthesis of public BAES text and NIST AI RMF 1.0 Core; ISO/IEC 42001:2023 remains UL at clause level unless authorized access changes;
- Phase 1 research report and appropriate public summary.

**Phase 2 — Conditional Deliverables**

Only if Phase 2 is approved and actually conducted:

- Reviewer responses and associated evidence references;
- Human-review interpretation analysis;
- Descriptive comparison across the four study conditions;
- Analysis of reviewer uncertainty and unresolved interpretations;
- Phase 2 report, including limitations and any protocol deviations.

Reviewer responses and human-performance findings are not current deliverables and must not be interpreted as existing evidence.

The precise deliverables and acceptance criteria will be agreed in advance.

### Funding, Payment Structure, and Duration

The proposed founding sponsorship is **USD 40,000**, with an approximate six-month initial planning envelope. It supports the agreed independent research and BAES development programme and includes a defined allocation for the founder's Research and Engineering Leadership. The planning period and work sequence may be adjusted by mutual written agreement; the amount is not a consulting fee, equity investment, or purchase price for BAES.

The proposed payment schedule is:

| Payment stage | Amount | Proposed payment condition |
|---|---:|---|
| Initial mobilization payment | USD 18,000 | Upon execution of the agreed agreement and before commencement of the programme |
| Intermediate milestone payment | USD 12,000 | Upon completion and acceptance of the agreed intermediate deliverables |
| Final milestone payment | USD 10,000 | Upon completion and acceptance of the agreed final deliverables |
| **Total** | **USD 40,000** | |

The milestone definitions, deliverable acceptance procedure, payment deadlines, and applicable administrative arrangements will be specified in the final agreement.

The initial mobilization payment forms part of the total programme funding; it is not an additional fee.

The proposed budget should transparently identify the founder's Research and Engineering Leadership allocation as well as the resources required for the agreed programme activities. The detailed allocation, responsibilities, milestones, and payment conditions will be defined in the sponsorship agreement.

The proposed budget distinguishes between:

**Phase 1 — Completion and Finalization Work**

- Final stabilization and version control of the S01 evidence packets and evidence index;
- Remaining scenario and criteria consistency review;
- Independent second-coder verification, if an appropriate reviewer is available;
- Source-bounded comparative synthesis and explicit treatment of access-limited material;
- Confidentiality, intellectual-property, licensing, privacy, and third-party-rights review;
- Final documentation and reporting.

**Phase 2 — Conditional Research Work**

- Independent reviewer honoraria;
- Reviewer recruitment and coordination;
- Human-participant study execution;
- Human-response analysis;
- Additional reporting and study administration.

Phase 2 expenses will be incurred only if the relevant prerequisites are satisfied and the study is authorized to proceed. Conditional funds should not automatically be treated as committed expenditures or silently reallocated.

If Phase 2 cannot proceed, any resulting change to the scope, deliverables, or allocation of funds will require an explicit written agreement between the parties.

The USD 40,000 amount is a proposed founding sponsorship figure, not a purchase price for BAES, a valuation of its intellectual property, an equity investment, or an automatic grant of ownership or exclusive rights. Any commercial-use rights or licensing permissions are negotiable only through a separate written agreement.

The final agreement will define the scope, resources, access requirements, deliverables, and payment obligations.

### Collaboration Model

A funding or research partner may:

- Propose a relevant research question or application domain;
- Contribute domain expertise and methodological feedback;
- Provide authorized access to appropriate systems, test environments, or evaluation materials;
- Support the active Phase 1 research;
- Help identify independent reviewers for the conditional Phase 2 study;
- Review the agreed scope, milestones, and deliverables;
- Designate an institutional coordination focal point to facilitate communication and coordination.

Any access to systems or confidential information will require appropriate authorization and agreed handling conditions.

Where useful and mutually agreed, the collaboration may include an in-person visit to the sponsor's country. The purpose, timing, travel arrangements, and associated costs of any visit will be defined separately in the written collaboration plan.

### Institutional Recognition and Research Sponsorship

Subject to a separate written agreement, an organization supporting the programme may be recognized as:

**Official Research Sponsor of the agreed BAES research and development programme.**

The recognition may include:

- Acknowledgment of the sponsor in selected programme materials and the final research report;
- Inclusion of the sponsor's approved name or logo on the agreed sponsorship or programme information page;
- Appropriate acknowledgment in selected public presentations or research summaries.

The exact recognition arrangements, use of names and logos, and publication permissions must be approved in writing.

Sponsor recognition does not imply ownership of BAES, control over its standardization decisions, endorsement of every technical provision, or authority over independent research conclusions.

### Early Access and Institutional Use

Subject to a separate written licence, an institutional partner may receive:

- Early access to agreed BAES materials and selected development outputs;
- Permission to use specified materials for internal research, technical assessment, and agreed institutional applications;
- An opportunity to discuss broader institutional licensing arrangements.

The scope, duration, permitted users, redistribution rights, confidentiality obligations, and any fees for institutional use will be defined in the applicable licence.

Exclusive rights, if considered, require a separate negotiation and agreement defining their scope, field of use, territory, duration, covered materials, future versions, modification rights, and redistribution conditions.

No exclusive or unrestricted licence is included automatically in the USD 40,000 research programme.

### Future Professional Education and Development

A future collaboration may explore a BAES professional education programme, potentially including:

- Train-the-Trainer programmes;

- Curriculum and assessment development;
- A structured professional certification pathway.

These are future opportunities, not existing certified services or guaranteed programme deliverables.

**BAES Lead Instructor and Programme Director.**

### Independence, Ownership, and Transparency

The evaluation must be conducted and reported according to the evidence obtained.

Funding does not guarantee a favorable result or permit a sponsor to dictate research conclusions, alter evaluation criteria without documented methodological justification, or suppress negative or inconclusive findings.

Ownership of BAES and its underlying intellectual property remains with its creator unless expressly agreed otherwise in a separate written instrument.

The research agreement will distinguish between:

- **Background intellectual property:** BAES, its existing architecture, principles, models, and pre-existing materials;
- **Programme outputs:** Reports, evidence indexes, assessments, and other deliverables specifically produced within the agreed research scope;
- **Permitted use:** The rights granted to the sponsor or institutional partner to use specified programme outputs and materials.

Any licence, publication, confidentiality, ownership, or permitted-use terms must be agreed in writing before the relevant activities begin.

Public reporting will distinguish observed findings from assumptions, interpretations, and unresolved questions.

### Why Collaborate?

This opportunity provides a way to investigate practical questions about human authority and authorized action in AI-assisted engineering through a bounded research project with documented methods, evidence, and limitations.

The immediate value to a partner is participation in the construction and assessment of a structured evidence-based research approach.

An institutional partner may also have the opportunity to establish a longer-term relationship with BAES through separate licensing, research, and professional education arrangements.

Any conclusions about human reviewer performance or the comparative effectiveness of review approaches depend on the successful completion of the separate Phase 2 study.

The project does not offer advance guarantees of validation, certification, superiority, or a predetermined result.
