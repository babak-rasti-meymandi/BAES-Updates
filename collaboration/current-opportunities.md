# Current Research and Collaboration Opportunities

This page lists current opportunities to fund, evaluate, and collaborate on the Babak AI Engineering Standard (BAES).

Each opportunity is subject to a clearly defined scope, agreed deliverables, and transparent terms. Listing an opportunity does not imply an existing partnership, funding commitment, or endorsement.

## Opportunity 01 — Independent Evaluation of Human Authorization in AI-Assisted Engineering


**Status:** Open for Discussion  
**Current research phase:** Phase 1 — Active  
**Next research phase:** Phase 2 — Deferred and Conditional  
**Proposed planning envelope:** Approximately six months  
**Indicative funding request:** USD 30,000

### Objective

Support an independent, evidence-based evaluation of how human intent, authorization, delegated AI actions, available evidence, and human decisions can be represented and reconstructed in AI-assisted engineering work.

The research compares BAES with selected established frameworks, including NIST AI RMF 1.0 and ISO/IEC 42001:2023.

The evaluation is designed to permit positive, negative, mixed, or inconclusive findings. It does not assume that BAES performs better than the comparison frameworks.

The current engagement prioritizes a bounded evidence-assessment task rather than attempting to establish universal validity across all domains.

### Current Research Scope

The project is organized into two distinct phases.

**Phase 1 — Evidence-Packet Construction and Investigator-Side Assessment**

**Status: Active**

The current work examines whether constructed evidence packets are internally coherent, traceable, and sufficiently informative to support reconstruction of authorization-related events and decisions.

The active scope includes:

1. Stabilizing the public synthetic S01 scenario and its C99 complete_groups technical baseline.
2. Constructing evidence variants representing explicit, traceable authorization and artifact approval where specific informed authorization remains unresolved.
3. Preparing an evidence index and supporting traceability references.
4. Mapping the preserved evaluation criteria C1–C20.
5. Conducting an investigator-side traceability and reconstruction audit.
6. Maintaining an ambiguity register documenting unresolved interpretations and evidential limitations.
7. Preparing a comparative assessment of BAES, NIST AI RMF 1.0, and ISO/IEC 42001:2023 using permitted source material.

The unit of assessment in Phase 1 is the evidence packet and its records, not human reviewer performance.

**Expected Phase 1 outputs:**

- Stabilized evidence packets and variants;
- Evidence index;
- C1–C20 mapping;
- Investigator-side traceability and reconstruction audit;
- Ambiguity register;
- Documented findings and limitations;
- Phase 1 research report.

**Phase 2 — Human Reviewer Evaluation**

**Status: Deferred and Conditional**

Phase 2 is a proposed human-participant study. It is not part of the currently active assessment.

The proposed design uses a held-out scenario, HDA-01, and a 2×2 between-reviewer comparison:

| Condition | Review approach | Decision evidence |
|---|---|---|
| A-R1 | Conventional review | Explicit traceable decision record |
| A-R2 | Conventional review | Artifact approval; specific informed authorization unresolved |
| B-R1 | Structured review | Explicit traceable decision record |
| B-R2 | Structured review | Artifact approval; specific informed authorization unresolved |

Each reviewer would receive one evidence packet only.

The public S01 scenario is not suitable for blinded confirmatory testing because its behavior and boundary-test result are already public. It may support exploratory usability work, but it will not be presented as a blinded confirmatory case.

Phase 2 can proceed only after:

- Phase 1 evidence packets and the evidence index are stabilized;
- HDA-01 and its associated materials are finalized and frozen;
- Private repository access controls and collaborator permissions are verified;
- The study protocol, reviewer assignment, instructions, coding, exposure rules, consent, privacy, and data-retention arrangements are defined and frozen;
- Independent reviewers are available.

The proposed target is eight independent reviewers, with two per condition. A minimum of four may support a limited feasibility pilot if all four conditions are represented. A smaller or incomplete sample would not support comparative condition-effect claims.

No human reviewer has been recruited or assessed to date, and no human-review data have been collected.

### Research Brief

The [Evaluation Brief](evaluation-brief.md) provides the detailed research question, phase-specific scope, comparison conditions, deliverables, proposed timeline, indicative budget, and independence safeguards.

The brief is a research proposal, not a frozen protocol, preregistered study, completed evaluation, or claim of validation.

### Proposed Scope of Collaboration

The immediate collaboration opportunity concerns Phase 1 and may include:

1. Supporting evidence-packet construction and stabilization.
2. Reviewing the research boundaries and evidence requirements.
3. Providing relevant engineering or research-methodology feedback.
4. Supporting the C1–C20 mapping and comparative framework assessment.
5. Reviewing the investigator-side reconstruction and traceability approach.
6. Supporting documentation of findings, uncertainties, and limitations.

Support for Phase 2 may be discussed separately, subject to completion of its prerequisites and an agreed research arrangement.

The final scope, responsibilities, deliverables, and acceptance criteria will be agreed with the funding or research partner before work begins.

### Expected Deliverables

**Phase 1 — Current Deliverables**

- Evidence packets and supporting technical materials;
- Evidence index and traceability references;
- C1–C20 mapping;
- Investigator-side reconstruction and evidence-sufficiency assessment;
- Ambiguity register;
- Comparative assessment and documented limitations;
- Phase 1 research report and appropriate public summary.

**Phase 2 — Conditional Deliverables**

Only if Phase 2 is approved and actually conducted:

- Reviewer responses and associated evidence references;
- Human-review interpretation analysis;
- Descriptive comparison across the four study conditions;
- Analysis of reviewer uncertainty and unresolved interpretations;
- Phase 2 report, including limitations and any protocol deviations.

Reviewer responses and human-performance findings are not current deliverables and must not be interpreted as existing evidence.

The precise deliverables and acceptance criteria will be agreed in advance.

### Funding and Duration

The initial proposal is approximately **USD 30,000 for a six-month planning envelope**.

This is an indicative amount, not a fixed quotation, existing funding commitment, or guarantee that both research phases will be completed within six months.

The proposed budget distinguishes between:

**Phase 1 — Active Research Work**

- Research and evidence-packet construction;
- Scenario and evidence stabilization;
- Evidence indexing and criteria mapping;
- Comparative assessment;
- Traceability and reconstruction audit;
- Documentation and reporting.

**Phase 2 — Conditional Research Work**

- Independent reviewer honoraria;
- Reviewer recruitment and coordination;
- Human-participant study execution;
- Human-response analysis;
- Additional reporting and study administration.

Phase 2 expenses will be incurred only if the relevant prerequisites are satisfied and the study is authorized to proceed. Conditional funds should not automatically be treated as committed expenditures or silently reallocated.

The final budget will depend on the agreed scope, resources, access requirements, and deliverables.

A smaller or larger engagement may be considered if it supports a coherent and clearly bounded research objective.

### Collaboration Model

A funding or research partner may:

- Propose a relevant research question or application domain;
- Contribute domain expertise and methodological feedback;
- Provide authorized access to appropriate systems, test environments, or evaluation materials;
- Support the active Phase 1 research;
- Help identify independent reviewers for the conditional Phase 2 study;
- Review the agreed scope, milestones, and deliverables.

Any access to systems or confidential information will require appropriate authorization and agreed handling conditions.

### Independence, Ownership, and Transparency

The evaluation must be conducted and reported according to the evidence obtained.

Funding does not guarantee a favorable result or permit a sponsor to dictate research conclusions, alter evaluation criteria without documented methodological justification, or suppress negative or inconclusive findings.

The engagement does not automatically transfer ownership of BAES or grant unrestricted rights to its use. Intellectual property, licensing, publication, confidentiality, and permitted use of project outputs must be agreed in writing before the engagement begins.

Public reporting will distinguish observed findings from assumptions, interpretations, and unresolved questions.

### Why Collaborate?

This opportunity provides a way to investigate practical questions about human authority and authorized action in AI-assisted engineering through a bounded research project with documented methods, evidence, and limitations.

The immediate value to a partner is participation in the construction and assessment of a structured evidence-based research approach.

Any conclusions about human reviewer performance or the comparative effectiveness of review approaches depend on the successful completion of the separate Phase 2 study.

The project does not offer advance guarantees of validation, certification, superiority, or a predetermined result.
