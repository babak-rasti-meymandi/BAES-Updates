# BAES Consulting — Authority & Evidence Design

<img src="../assets/images/12-human-at-the-center.svg" alt="Human at the center" width="96" />

**Make human authorization explicit. Make engineering decisions traceable.**

BAES consulting is a proposed, independent advisory service for organizations that use or are considering AI-assisted engineering. It focuses on a practical question:

> **What has a human actually authorized an AI system to do, and what evidence is needed to justify the resulting engineering decision?**

This page is designed for a quick first read. Start with the summary below; the detailed scope and limitations follow.

## The problem this addresses

<img src="../assets/images/07-decision-making.svg" alt="Decision making" width="96" />

AI-assisted engineering can produce changes that are technically plausible yet extend beyond the human's stated intent. A passing test suite, a completed code review, or a merge approval may be relevant evidence—but none automatically proves that every material behavior change was knowingly authorized.

Organizations may therefore need clearer answers to questions such as:

- What outcome and boundaries did the human specify?
- Which actions may the AI perform without further approval?
- Which changes require disclosure or renewed authorization?
- What evidence supports the conclusion that a change was authorized?
- How should uncertainty, disagreement, or missing evidence be recorded?

The consulting starts with a specific organizational or engineering question. It does not assume that every organization needs the same policy or process.

## What the consulting covers

<img src="../assets/images/08-governance-compliance.svg" alt="Governance and compliance" width="96" />

The exact scope is agreed in advance. Depending on the problem, the work may cover:

- **Human intent and authorized scope** — make the intended outcome, permitted changes, and explicit constraints easier to identify.
- **Delegated AI action** — distinguish actions an AI system may carry out from decisions that remain under human authority.
- **Approval and authorization** — clarify what a review, approval, or merge decision does and does not establish.
- **Evidence requirements** — map important claims and decisions to the evidence needed to support them.
- **Deviation handling** — define how out-of-scope changes should be disclosed, reviewed, escalated, and authorized where appropriate.
- **Traceability and accountability** — connect instructions, changes, evidence, human decisions, and subsequent actions.
- **Human override** — examine how human intervention and decision authority are represented in the agreed context.

This is advisory work. Implementation, system access, formal testing, or independent evaluation is included only if explicitly agreed.

## What you can receive

<img src="../assets/images/10-evaluation-evidence.svg" alt="Evaluation and evidence" width="96" />

Deliverables are selected to match the agreed question. Possible outputs include:

| Deliverable | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **Authority and responsibility map** | Identifies human decision rights, delegated AI actions, approval boundaries, and escalation paths. |
| **Authorization-scope specification** | Records intended outcomes, permitted changes, constraints, and conditions that require renewed authorization. |
| **Evidence matrix** | Connects key claims and decisions to the evidence needed to support them. |
| **Decision or deviation record template** | Helps document what changed, what was disclosed, what was authorized, by whom, and on what evidence. |
| **Gap analysis** | Identifies ambiguities, missing evidence, conflicting responsibilities, and weaknesses in the current arrangement. |
| **Written recommendations** | Provides context-specific options, rationale, and implementation considerations. |
| **Optional evaluation plan** | Defines scenarios, criteria, evidence, and limitations for a possible subsequent evaluation. |

Not every engagement needs every deliverable. The final list and acceptance criteria will be written into the agreed scope.

## How an engagement works

<img src="../assets/images/01-institutions-organizations.svg" alt="Institutions and organizations" width="96" />

**A straightforward, written-first process:**

1. **Describe the problem.** Share a high-level description of the engineering context and the authorization or evidence question. Do not send credentials, confidential code, or restricted data in an initial public message.
2. **Check fit.** Establish whether the question is suitable for this advisory service.
3. **Define the scope.** Agree on the problem statement, assumptions, access requirements, exclusions, deliverables, and acceptance criteria.
4. **Agree on terms.** Confirm the fee, schedule, payment arrangements, confidentiality, intellectual-property terms, and any publication restrictions in writing.
5. **Carry out the work.** Work against the agreed scope and record relevant assumptions and limitations.
6. **Hand over the results.** Provide the agreed written outputs and discuss unresolved questions or possible next steps.

No work begins until the scope and terms have been agreed. Initial discussion can take place in writing; a call is not a prerequisite to making an initial inquiry.

## Pricing and terms

**Pricing is quoted after the scope is defined.** There is no universal price on this page.

The quotation will reflect the agreed deliverables, expected effort, complexity, required access, schedule, and any specialist review or testing. A bounded engagement may be suitable where the question and outputs can be clearly specified. Larger engagements may be divided into phases and quoted separately.

The separate research proposal requests an indicative **USD 30,000 over six months** for a proposed independent evaluation. That is a research funding estimate—not the price of this consulting service, a rate card, or a commitment to fund or undertake commercial work.

## Independence and limitations

BAES is an independently developed, technology-neutral standard. Consulting recommendations will be based on the agreed context and the evidence available.

- No favorable conclusion is guaranteed.
- This service is **not certification, regulatory approval, or a guarantee of AI-system safety**.
- Advice does not replace the client's engineering judgment, legal advice, or required organizational approvals.
- Findings will distinguish observations from interpretations, assumptions, and unresolved questions.
- No claim is made here that this consulting approach has been independently validated across organizations, systems, or engineering domains.

The service is advisory unless additional responsibilities are explicitly agreed in writing.

## Intellectual property and confidentiality

The engagement does not automatically transfer ownership of BAES. Before work begins, the parties will agree in writing on:

- pre-existing intellectual property and applicable licenses;
- ownership and permitted use of engagement-specific deliverables;
- confidentiality and handling of customer information;
- authorized access to code, systems, records, and other materials;
- whether any findings may be published and under what conditions.

Use or redistribution of public BAES materials remains subject to their applicable licenses and other relevant rights. Confidential customer material will not be published without appropriate authorization.
